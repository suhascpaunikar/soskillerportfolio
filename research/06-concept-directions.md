# 06 — Three concept directions (and a recommendation)

Every direction below follows the non-negotiables in `02-hiring-insights.md`: a 10-second pitch, a 30-second version of every case study, playable proof, reduced-motion by default, and mobile that works on a mid-range Android.

The test for each idea: **only you could own it, and it makes your content faster to understand, not slower.**

---

## A. "Director's Cut": your portfolio is an edit  ★ recommended

**One-liner:** *A product designer who used to cut films for Netflix, Prime and Zakir Khan presents his work the way an editor would: as cuts, test screenings and box-office numbers.*

### Why it's yours
- You literally were a video editor (OML, 2019–21). Your old bio said *"journeys where the flow does the storytelling."*
- Your case studies *already have a film structure*: BOPIS is **rough cut (V1) → test screening (10% A/B, −10% conversion) → audience feedback (user calls) → re-edit (V2) → box office (23 in 100, 2.5× fewer returns)**. The AI project has **a pilot episode (1 month, 2 stores) → renewed for the full season (live in every store)**.
- The metaphor gives you a coherent *system* of interactions (scrubbing, cuts, grades, credits), not just a skin.

### First 10 seconds (desktop)
A dark, cinematic editing-suite layout. A big **program monitor** loops a 6-second clip of your best work, with a title card over it:
> **"I used to cut films. Now I cut checkout flows."**
> Product designer · commerce, retail & AI tools · ex-Lenskart · 23 in 100 orders now picked up in store
Under it, a **timeline**: V1 (shipped work), V2 (lab), A1 (your story as a waveform). A red **● REC** dot and a running timecode in the nav. Buttons: *Watch the 30-sec cut* · *Copy email* · *Résumé*.

### Section mapping
| Site part | Film language |
|---|---|
| Home hero | Program monitor + title card |
| Project index | Timeline tracks with clips (drag the playhead, J/K/L, ← →) |
| Case study | "The Cut", with a **30-sec cut / Director's cut** toggle |
| V1 → V2 | Rough cut → Test screening → Re-edit |
| Results | Box office |
| Explorations that didn't ship | Deleted scenes |
| Process | Behind the scenes |
| Reflection | Director's commentary (optional voice track) |
| Team | Credits |
| About | **"Previously on…"**, a season-by-season recap: Event Capital → Pinstorm → OML (Netflix/Prime/Zakir Khan) → Saoji Spice → Ting → Lenskart → Now |
| Motion control | **CBFC certificate**: U · U/A · A (or the 🌶 Spice level) |
| Theme switcher | Colour grade: *Daylight* / *Night shoot* / *Bleach bypass* |
| Footer | End credits rolling real collaborators' names, then a post-credits scene |
| 404 | "Scene missing" |
| Mobile | **Reels**: vertical, full-bleed clips you swipe through, which is how people already watch on phones |

### Visual language
- **Colour:** graphite base (`#0E0E0F` / `#17171A`) with Cloud Dancer text (`#F0EEE9`, Pantone 2026) and **one accent: REC red** (`#FF3B30`), or a Saoji chilli red-orange (`#E8431C`) for the spice joke. A light *Daylight* grade for readability fans.
- **Type:** an editorial serif for loglines and title cards (Instrument Serif, free), mono for timecodes and metadata (Geist Mono or JetBrains Mono), and an Indian-made grotesk for UI and body (General Sans or Satoshi from ITF's Fontshare). A tiny Devanagari signature (सुहास) in Mukta.
- **Texture:** subtle film grain; letterbox bars on case-study heroes.
- **Motion:** *hard cuts* rather than fades (snappy, cinematic, cheap); playhead on a spring; split-text title cards; UI motion under 300ms, storytelling moments longer.

### Risks → mitigations
- *Looks like a tool for video editors, not a product designer* → the hero copy and the 30-sec cut put product outcomes first; the metaphor frames your work rather than replacing it.
- *Dark UI and readability* → AA contrast tokens; a Daylight grade; case-study body text on a light "script page" surface.
- *Horizontal timeline accessibility* → the timeline is a separate control with buttons and links; page scroll stays vertical; full keyboard support.
- *Video weight* → short, muted, compressed loops with poster frames; lazy-load everything below the fold.

**Effort:** M–L (3–5 weeks part-time). **Signature references:** CUTT and Timecode templates, Filmstrip Scrub, Robby Leonardi (career as levels), Bruno Simon (one metaphor, total commitment).

## B. "Checkout": your portfolio is a store

**One-liner:** *The designer behind Lenskart's store-pickup checkout presents himself as a beautifully designed product page, and every interaction is a work sample of commerce UX.*

### Why it's yours
- Your strongest shipped work is checkout and fulfilment, and you've also designed POS and in-store tools. The medium *is* the message.
- Precedent that it works: Philippe Dubost's Amazon-page CV (2013) got ~1.5M visits and a job within months. You'd do it with 2026 craft and your own patterns.

### First 10 seconds
A premium, playful product page (think Teenage Engineering, not Amazon clutter):
> **Suhas Paunikar — Product Designer** · 4+ yrs · ★ reviews from real teammates
> *In stock. Available from {date}.* · **Delivery:** Remote ✓ · Relocation ✓ · **Pick up in office:** {city}
> **Spice level:** Mild · Medium · Saoji 🌶 (that's the motion-intensity control)
> **Specs:** commerce & checkout · retail/POS · AI tools · Figma · Framer · Claude Code
> [ **Add to cart** ] [ **Copy email** ] [ **Résumé** ]

### Section mapping
| Site part | Store language |
|---|---|
| Hero | Product detail page |
| Flagship case studies | **Bestsellers** (each case study is a PDP: "what's in the box", specs = metrics, reviews = user quotes) |
| Lab / vibe-coded | **New arrivals** (Fidle.app as a "limited edition") |
| Journey | **Order history**, past orders marked *Delivered*: OML "{n} edits for Netflix, Prime, Zakir Khan" (your real number), Saoji Spice "Kitchen closed, lessons kept"… *Next order: your team* |
| Testimonials | **Ratings & reviews** (real quotes only) |
| FAQ | **Questions & answers** (notice period, relocation, how you use AI) |
| Contact | **Checkout**: the cart lists the projects they shortlisted; "Home delivery" = email; "Pickup" = book a call |
| After contact | **Order tracking**: received → reading → reply on the way (your own BOPIS order-card pattern) |
| Easter eggs | Coupon **HIRESUHAS**; "COD not available" (a nod to the BOPIS constraint) |

### Visual language
Light and premium: Cloud Dancer base, ink text, **one accent** (chilli red), mono for SKUs and specs, product-style "renders" of objects tied to your work (frames, iPad, order slip), crisp shadows, satisfying add-to-cart physics.

### Risks → mitigations
- *Selling yourself literally can feel gimmicky or cringe* → keep the humour in micro-copy; keep the case studies serious; no fake ratings, ever.
- *Pigeonholes you as "the commerce guy"* → fine if you want commerce roles; a limitation if you're targeting AI/B2B roles.
- *A 2013 idea* → yes, but nobody has done it as a real checkout designer with real checkout patterns.

**Effort:** M (2–4 weeks). **References:** Dubost's CV, Teenage Engineering, your own BOPIS screens.

## C. "Studio Desk": your portfolio is a desk of objects

**One-liner:** *A top-down desk covered in real objects from your work and life. Pick one up to open its story, or hit "Tidy up" and it all snaps into a neat index.*

### Why it's yours
- The objects tell your story better than a table: **spectacles** (AI measurements), an **order slip** (BOPIS), an **iPad**, a **film reel / SD card** (OML), a **spice tin** (Saoji Spice), a **phone** (concepts), **sticky notes** (lab).
- It rides two strong 2026 trends: *trinket/catalogue design* (objects as identity) and *blueprint design* (annotated, deconstructed spec sheets fit "systems thinking").

### First 10 seconds
A warm desk surface with soft shadows, objects tagged with blueprint-style numbered labels, a one-line intro on a sticky note, and a **"Tidy up ↦ list view"** button for recruiters. The lamp follows the visitor's local time (morning light or evening lamp). That doubles as a diegetic dark mode.

### Risks → mitigations
- *Discoverability* → visible labels plus a list view one click away.
- *Mobile* → the desk becomes a vertical "drawer" of object cards.
- *Build cost* → physics and photography of real objects take time (L).
- *Closest to existing work* (OS/desk portfolios like Rauno's, Mariano Pascual's, Poolsuite) → less "only-you" than A or B.

**Effort:** L (4–6 weeks). **References:** Mariano Pascual, Poolsuite, Framer Desk/Deskfolio, Physics Sticker Wall, Kittl Trinket and Blueprint.

## Scorecard

Weights follow your brief ("fun, engaging, stand out immediately"): uniqueness ×2, fun ×1.5, 10-second clarity ×1.5, everything else ×1. Scores are 1–5 (higher is better; for gimmick risk, 5 = low risk).

| Criterion (weight) | A · Director's Cut | B · Checkout | C · Studio Desk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Only-you uniqueness (×2) | **5** | 3.5 | 3 |
| Fun / replay (×1.5) | **5** | 4 | **5** |
| 10-second clarity (×1.5) | 4 | **5** | 3 |
| Relevance to roles you'll apply for (×1) | 4 | 4 | 3 |
| Mobile quality (×1) | 4.5 | **5** | 2 |
| Build effort (×1, 5 = easiest) | 3 | **4** | 2 |
| Low gimmick risk (×1) | **4** | 3 | 3 |
| **Weighted total** | **39** | 36.5 | 28 |

**Recommendation: A, "Director's Cut".** It's the most *only-you* idea, it turns your case studies into a story structure that's *easier* to follow (rough cut → test screening → re-edit → box office), it suits your love of visual effects, and it works on mobile as reels. **B is a close second.** If you weight clarity and speed-to-launch over uniqueness, B wins, and it's the sharper choice if you're targeting commerce roles specifically. **C** is the most playful but the riskiest and slowest. Keep it as a mini-game or an About-page moment rather than the whole site.

**Update (2 Nov 2026):** A was chosen. In the wireframes the home became the editing app itself (one screen, where every clip opens its page), and on phones a phone video editor replaced reels. See [`07-site-structure.md`](07-site-structure.md).

> Whatever you pick, **don't mix metaphors** across the site. Borrow *mechanics* freely (the shortlist cart, the spice knob, the desk "tidy up"), but re-skin them in the chosen language.
